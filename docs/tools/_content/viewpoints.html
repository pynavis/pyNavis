The Viewpoints panel looks after saved views, the section box, view state and navigation speeds: the things that decide whether a review model is pleasant to walk or a chore.

What it does

The tool for a viewpoint tree that arrived from three people with three naming conventions. Tick the views, choose an operation, watch the preview, apply.

How to use it

  1. Click Rename and tick the viewpoints to change.
  2. Choose an operation: find and replace, plain or regular expression; a prefix and a suffix; sequential numbering with a start and a padding; or a case cleanup.
  3. Read the preview. Collisions, two views ending up with the same name, are flagged as you type, and nothing applies while one remains.
  4. Click Apply. The toast counts what was renamed.

Limitations

  • Folders are renamed like viewpoints, but their contents are not renumbered as a unit; tick the children for that.

Other uses

  • Number a folder of views in walk order before exporting a report, so the pages come out in sequence.
  • Strip a date suffix off fifty views in one go.

What it does

Navisworks deletes several viewpoints at once too: Ctrl or Shift click them in the Saved Viewpoints window and press Delete. This is for when finding them is the work. Search by name, or tick every animation, every view that shares its name with another, or every view carrying comments, check the count, and delete them as one operation.

How to use it

  1. Click Delete. Search or use the pickers to tick what should go.
  2. Confirm. The dialog names how many items will be removed, because this changes the document.
  3. The toast reports what was removed and anything that failed.

Limitations

  • Deleting is one undo step in Navisworks, so Ctrl+Z works, but check the count in the confirmation first.

Other uses

  • Clear out every view with a comment after the comments have been actioned.

What it does

Where Rename fixes names, this fixes structure. The dialog stays open and applies each action as you choose it, re-reading the tree afterwards so the next action is never planned against a stale picture.

How to use it

  1. Click Manage.
  2. Sort the tree A to Z, move ticked items into a folder, create a folder, or purge folders that hold no viewpoints. Each applies at once.

Limitations

  • A move that is not legal, such as a folder into itself, is refused with a reason rather than half applied.
  • Purge asks for confirmation naming how many folders it would remove.

What it does

Navisworks' own Fit Section to Selection gives a box square to the world, so a diagonal run of pipe gets four corners of empty space. This gives planes parallel to the run, with a little padding so faces do not graze the geometry.

How to use it

  1. Select the objects.
  2. Click Section Fit. The toast reports the box it made.

Limitations

  • Inside the snap angle, 1.5 degrees by default, the box stays square to the world, because a small tilt costs every plane's alignment and buys nothing.
  • A selection with no geometry, such as a folder node, has nothing to fit.

Other uses

  • Section to one room's worth of services before a walkthrough, then nudge the faces with Section Nudge.

Good to know

The padding starts at 150 mm and is shown and typed in the document's units, like any pyNavis length: 6" in a feet-and-inches document, or a value with its unit such as 150mm anywhere. Both settings are shared with Section Plan in section_planes.json.

What it does

Fits the planes the same way Section Fit does, then swings the camera to an orthographic plan of them. The camera turns with the box, so the objects sit square on screen rather than skewed across it.

How to use it

  1. Select the objects and click Section Plan.

Good to know

Plan has no Shift+Click of its own; change the padding and snap angle from Section Fit.

What it does

The answer to setting up a section, a set of hidden items or a colour scheme and then needing to look at something else. Pick a kind of state and it is written under %APPDATA%\pyNavis\viewstate\ keyed to the document, so it is still there in another session.

How to use it

  1. Click Copy State and pick section, hidden items or appearance.
  2. Change whatever you need to.
  3. Click Paste State to put it back.

Limitations

  • One copy of each kind per document. Copying section again replaces the earlier copy.

Other uses

  • Copy the appearance overrides of a colour-coded review, clear them to take clean screenshots, paste them back.

What it does

Offers only the kinds actually copied for this document and restores the one you pick. With nothing copied yet it says so instead of showing an empty picker.

What it does

Navisworks resets navigation speed constantly: every recalled viewpoint brings its own, and a model in the wrong units makes walking either a crawl or a sprint. This puts your numbers back.

How to use it

  1. Shift+Click once to set the three values. The defaults are 30 m/s, 45 degrees per second and 84 degrees. Untick any a reset should leave alone.
  2. Whenever walking feels wrong, press Alt+Q.

Limitations

  • Field of view needs a perspective view. In an orthographic one it warns and applies the two speeds only.

Good to know

The unit dropdown converts the number rather than relabelling it, so switching m/s to ft/s turns 30 into 98.4252. The speed is stored in the unit you typed and converted to the document's units when applied, so it means the same thing in a model drawn in feet.

What it does

A set of views handed over by someone else navigates the way they liked. This rewrites the ticked views with your numbers, each one copied, its camera edited and swapped back, so nothing is loaded and the whole run is one undo step.

How to use it

  1. Press Alt+Z or click Speeds to Saved. The values about to be written are named at the top of the browser.
  2. Tick views or whole folders and click OK. The progress window can stop the run part way, keeping what was written.

Limitations

  • Folders have no camera and animations keep theirs on their own cuts; both are counted and skipped.
  • On some Navisworks releases a view has to be rebuilt to take the change, and the toast says plainly when redlines and appearance overrides were not carried over.

What it does

Two lines, live. The top line reads "Active viewpoint" while the camera still sits where the saved view put it and "Last viewpoint" once you have walked off. The bottom line is the view's name, sized to fit the panel. Click the name to go back.

How to use it

  1. Click Viewpoint Tracker on the ribbon to show the panel. Dock it wherever suits; the button follows the panel's visibility.
  2. Recall views as usual. The panel updates within a quarter of a second of the camera settling.

Limitations

  • Before any saved view has been current it shows "No viewpoint selected".
  • View > Windows lists the panel by its slot name, "pyNavis Panel N", not by its title.

Other uses

  • Presenting: the audience can see which view is on screen without you saying so.
  • Tracker Window in the panel flyout is the same two lines as a floating always-on-top window, for when the dock panel is in the way.

What it does

Dragging a section handle is imprecise and easy to overshoot. This panel moves the box or one of its faces a known distance per press, every press one undo step, from buttons or from the keyboard.

Adjust: one face at a time

The six faces sit in an unfolded box and are named the way you see them: Front is the face looking at you, Left is on the screen's left. The names follow the camera, so orbit round the model and Front is still the face in front of you; the small axis label under each name tells you which world axis that currently is. Click a face, then In to keep less of the model or Out to keep more. A tilted plane, a switched-off one, or a second plane facing the same way is listed under the remote by number.

Move: the whole box

The Move tab slides all six planes together: left, right, up, down on the screen, nearer or farther from you. Two frames to choose from. World axes snaps each move to the world axis nearest the screen direction, like the compass ring round the ViewCube, so the box stays on the building's grid. Screen follows the camera exactly, like the cube itself, so Left is always screen-left even from an odd angle.

The step

Halve and double buttons, presets in the document's units, and a box to type a value. On an imperial document the box reads and writes feet and inches the way Revit does: 0.5 is half a foot and shows as 0' 6", 0 0 1/8 is an eighth of an inch, and 1' 6", 6 1/2", 18" and 1-6 all work.

Keys and chords

  • With the panel focused: 0 to 6 pick a target, Tab cycles; Up and Down nudge a face; arrows move the box across the screen and Page Up and Page Down bring it nearer or push it farther; plus and minus change the step; Enter applies a typed step.
  • Over the 3D view, no panel needed: Ctrl+Alt with Up and Down nudge, with Left and Right change the target, with Page Up and Page Down double and halve the step. Each is listed in the Shortcuts editor under its Nudge title, so it can be moved off Ctrl+Alt on a machine where the display driver owns those keys.

Limitations

  • In Box mode, shrinking a face past the opposite face is refused.
  • The readout follows changes made elsewhere once a second, so a handle dragged in the view shows up after a beat.

Other uses

  • Fit to selection and Sectioning off sit at the bottom of the panel, so a whole sectioning job stays in one place.
  • Step through a building floor by floor: set the step to the storey height and press Up.

What it does

The same two lines as the Viewpoint Tracker panel in a floating window that stays on top, for when the dock panel is in the way or you are walking the model with another application on screen. Both run the same code, so they can never disagree.

How to use it

  1. Open the Viewpoints panel flyout and click Tracker Window.
  2. Click the name to go back to that view. Clicking the button again focuses the window that is already open rather than opening a second.
